          READ THIS BEFORE THE HAMMER

          The whole game.

          Six in. One still up.

          Choose a $2, $10 or $20 room, paid in PIT. Six fighters enter. Funded bots can fill the empty seats. Without enough funding, the room waits.

          The server locks the fighters and their plans, then calculates the match. You watch it unfold. Last survivor wins. The shrinking yard leaves exactly one survivor within 60 seconds.

          A K-hole takes a fighter out of this match. They come back next time. Closing your browser does not cancel a match.

          Follow the shiny things.

          20% of the room's PIT becomes pickups. Keep what you collect, even after a K-hole. First place receives 50%, second receives 25%, and 5% covers the house cut.

          Uncollected pickup PIT goes to first and second place in a two-to-one split. House bot winnings return to their reserve.

          Read a room's final receipt to see all six places and every PIT amount. The animation finishing and the chain confirming payments are separate steps.

          The big one.

          Each finished paid arena has a 1 in 200 chance to unlock the jackpot. One paying human in that room is then chosen at random. Place does not matter. House bots cannot win it.

          The first ten jackpots start with 1 million PIT each. Later jackpots start with 100,000 PIT. Every completed arena adds more: $1 for arenas 1–200, $0.50 for 201–400, then $0.10 each, converted to PIT at the room's price.

          This count is shared across all rooms and never resets after a win. The reserve is finite. A promised prize stays funded; fresh jackpots stop when there is no money to open them.

          Your plan. Their problem.

          Leave the note blank for a free random plan. A brain can turn your written note into habits. Your fighter still reacts to the yard and the people nearby.

          IQ changes how closely a fighter follows its plan, within small limits. It does not make a win certain. Rare clothes never give extra power.

          Every fighter charges one ultimate spell per match. Pick one of the ten spells in My fighter, or keep Surprise me and the yard picks for you when you enter. Heals, shields, heavy hits, snares, a shockwave, a beam, a pull and falling scrap all exist; each spell card states its exact numbers. Watch the purple bar and the marked warning area. An early K-hole can stop the cast. Spells respect accepted targeting rules; without a legal target, an attack spell becomes a temporary shield.

          Pictures from approved collections need proof of ownership. The Shed is for these fighters only, when it appears. Other objects explain their effects when you tap them.

          Who holds the strings?

          The game server calculates and signs the result. This version trusts that server. The payment contract limits where the room's PIT can go and prevents collecting twice.

          If a room cannot finish its payment steps before its recovery deadline, the contract can return the original PIT deposits. A lost connection or a lost fight is not a refund.

          Check the game network before signing. Testnet coins are for testing, not real-money prizes.

          Six little weirdos in a hole. This is a game, not a life plan.

          Assume it goes to zero.

          Treat every coin you put into a sock, an arena or PIT as money you may never see again. Be ready to lose the lot.

          No guaranteed value. No promised return. Never put in money you cannot afford to lose. If losing it would hurt, keep it out of the pit.
